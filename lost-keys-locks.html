Keys & Digital Locks

Keys, batteries, access and replacement issues.

Important: This guide is for general information, not legal, tax or professional advice. Rules may change, and your signed agreement and circumstances matter. Check official sources before acting. Read the full disclaimer →
THE SIMPLE VERSION

Record the number of keys, cards and remotes handed over. For digital locks, clarify routine battery replacement and report faults early rather than waiting until access is lost.

01

Start with the document

Check the signed tenancy agreement, inventory and any written amendments or messages that form part of the arrangement. The exact facts and wording matter.

02

Keep evidence

Keep relevant photos, receipts, payment records and messages. Good records make it much easier to establish what happened and what was agreed.

03

Resolve it clearly

Communicate the issue and proposed solution clearly. If both sides agree to change an existing arrangement, record that agreement in writing.

JC'S PRACTICAL TIP

Don't rely on what is “usually done” when the signed agreement says something specific. Read the clause first, then deal with the actual situation.

REAL RENTAL SITUATION

Who pays when an access card goes missing?

Only one gate remote is handed over, although the inventory lists two. Months later the landlord asks the tenant to replace the missing remote.

What I would check next

  1. Count keys, remotes and access cards at move-in and move-out.
  2. Record shortages immediately in the signed inventory.
  3. Check MCST replacement fees and authorisation requirements.
  4. Confirm whether the missing item was supplied before allocating replacement costs.

JC's takeaway: Check the applicable rules and signed agreement, then confirm decisions in writing.

REAL-LIFE RENTAL QUESTION

Who pays for a lost access card or faulty digital lock?

A tenant loses an access card while a digital lock fails from ordinary use.

What I would check first

Check the inventory and relevant clause; contact MCST for replacement-card rules; assess whether the lock failed through wear, batteries or misuse.

JC's practical takeaway: Record exactly which keys, remotes and access cards were handed over.
