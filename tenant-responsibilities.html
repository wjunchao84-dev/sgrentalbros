Tenant's Responsibilities During Tenancy

A practical checklist for looking after your rented home in Singapore.

Important: This guide is general educational information, not legal or professional advice. Some duties come from Singapore regulations; others depend on your signed tenancy agreement (TA). A sample tenancy clause is not automatically part of your agreement. Read the full disclaimer →
IN PLAIN ENGLISH

Pay your rent, care for the home, report problems early, and follow the terms you actually agreed to. Know the difference between a legal requirement and a contractual responsibility.

Official references checked: 10 October 2026 · Singapore

Who pays when something breaks?

It depends on the cause and the TA. A tenant may be responsible for damage caused by misuse or negligence. Ordinary wear and tear, major replacements and existing defects may be treated differently. The negotiated minor-repair clause can also allocate costs.

For example, if an aircon stops cooling, report the fault and provide your servicing records. Do not assume either party must pay until the cause and applicable clause are checked. If a contractor is needed, follow the agreed approval process.

A simple routine to follow

Every month

Pay rent on time, check utilities, keep the unit clean and report problems before they worsen.

When servicing is due

Arrange aircon or other maintenance only as required by your TA. Keep the receipts.

When something changes

Check with the landlord before adding occupants, subletting, keeping a new pet or altering the property.

Before moving out

Review the handover, cleaning, reinstatement and notice provisions. Arrange a condition check and return all keys and access devices.

JC’S PRACTICAL TIP

Keep one folder containing your signed TA, inventory photos, aircon servicing receipts, rent transfers and written repair approvals. It makes discussions much easier if a problem arises—and protects both landlord and tenant.
