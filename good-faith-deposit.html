Good Faith Deposit

Understand the payment before signing.

Important: This guide is for general information, not legal, tax or professional advice. Rules may change, and your signed agreement and circumstances matter. Check official sources before acting. Read the full disclaimer →
THE SIMPLE VERSION

A payment made during negotiations should be documented clearly. Check what the payment represents, when it may be applied toward another sum and what happens if the transaction does not proceed.

01

Start with the document

Check the signed tenancy agreement, inventory and any written amendments or messages that form part of the arrangement. The exact facts and wording matter.

02

Keep evidence

Keep relevant photos, receipts, payment records and messages. Good records make it much easier to establish what happened and what was agreed.

03

Resolve it clearly

Communicate the issue and proposed solution clearly. If both sides agree to change an existing arrangement, record that agreement in writing.

PRACTICAL RENTAL SCENARIO

What if negotiations fail after paying a deposit?

A tenant pays to reserve a unit, then disputes a proposed clause.

What to do next

  1. Get written payment instructions and receipt.
  2. Read refund and forfeiture terms.
  3. Verify the recipient before paying.

JC’s practical takeaway: The agreement and facts determine what happens to the money.

JC'S PRACTICAL TIP

Don't rely on what is “usually done” when the signed agreement says something specific. Read the clause first, then deal with the actual situation.

REAL RENTAL SITUATION

What happens to the good-faith deposit if the deal falls through?

A tenant transfers money to reserve a unit but later disagrees with the proposed tenancy terms. Both parties assume different refund rules.

What I would check next

  1. Verify the recipient and payment instructions independently before transferring.
  2. Record in writing whether the payment is refundable, forfeitable or credited toward rent or security deposit.
  3. Clarify what happens if the landlord rejects the offer or either side withdraws.
  4. Keep transfer proof and the accepted LOI or written offer.

JC's takeaway: Keep the agreement, supporting records and decisions clear in writing. Common market practice is not automatically a legal requirement.

REAL-LIFE RENTAL QUESTION

What happens if negotiations fail after money is paid?

A tenant transfers a booking sum before both sides agree on a diplomatic clause and then wants it back.

What I would check first

Get written confirmation of the purpose of payment; identify conditions for refund or forfeiture; pay only to a verified recipient; retain receipts.

JC's practical takeaway: A good-faith deposit is not governed by one universal refund rule for every tenancy.
