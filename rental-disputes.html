Rental Disputes

Document the facts and know the escalation options.

Important: This guide is for general information, not legal, tax or professional advice. Rules may change, and your signed agreement and circumstances matter. Check official sources before acting. Read the full disclaimer →
IN PLAIN ENGLISH

For a rental disagreement, first identify the exact clause, gather evidence and set out the resolution you are asking for. The correct formal dispute channel depends on the type of tenancy, claim and parties involved.

Sources checked: 9 October 2026 · Singapore

01

Gather the records

Keep the signed tenancy agreement and amendments, inventory, dated photographs, messages, invoices, payment receipts and a clear timeline. State the amount or remedy sought and why.

02

Try to resolve it in writing

Explain the relevant clause and evidence calmly, and give the other party a reasonable opportunity to respond. Record any settlement and the agreed payment or handover date.

03

When can Small Claims Tribunals help?

The Singapore Courts state that the Small Claims Tribunals can hear eligible disputes involving residential tenancy agreements of no more than two years. The usual claim limit is S$20,000, or S$30,000 with a signed Memorandum of Consent. Other eligibility rules and filing deadlines apply.

04

What about CEA?

CEA regulates property agencies and salespersons; it does not decide ordinary landlord–tenant contractual disputes. Depending on the dispute, mediation or legal advice may be more appropriate.

PRACTICAL RENTAL SCENARIO

What if landlord and tenant cannot agree?

A deposit dispute escalates through emotional messages without clear evidence.

What to do next

  1. Prepare a factual timeline and photos.
  2. Compare invoices and signed clauses.
  3. Consider suitable mediation or legal processes.

JC’s practical takeaway: Evidence and measured communication help more than accusations.

JC’S PRACTICAL TIP

Before escalating, write a one-page timeline with dates, clause references, evidence and the exact solution requested. It saves time and reduces confusion.

REAL RENTAL SITUATION

The landlord and tenant cannot agree on a deposit deduction. What next?

The landlord claims $600 for cleaning and repairs; the tenant disputes the amount and asks for the full deposit back.

What I would check next

  1. Identify the exact tenancy clause and gather dated photos, inventory and messages.
  2. Ask for itemised costs and try a written, specific settlement proposal.
  3. Check whether mediation or the Small Claims Tribunals is suitable for the type and value of dispute.
  4. Observe applicable claim limits and deadlines from the Singapore Courts' current guidance.

JC's takeaway: Verify current official requirements and preserve a clear written record. This is general information, not legal advice.

REAL-LIFE RENTAL QUESTION

The landlord and tenant disagree over a deduction. What now?

Both parties argue over cleaning and repair charges without a shared inventory or itemised invoice.

What I would check first

Gather the agreement, photos, messages and invoices; propose a written breakdown; attempt reasonable resolution; check the correct dispute-resolution forum and eligibility.

JC's practical takeaway: A calm timeline of evidence is usually more useful than a long argument over WhatsApp.
