End-of-Tenancy Timeline: From Notice to Deposit Refund

A practical Singapore rental move-out guide for tenants and landlords.

Important: This is a suggested planning timeline, not a set of statutory notice or cleaning deadlines. Your signed tenancy agreement (TA) determines many obligations. Always check the exact terms and applicable Singapore regulations. Read the full disclaimer →
IN PLAIN ENGLISH

Plan early, agree the handover process, compare the inventory fairly, document the keys and condition, then settle the deposit transparently.

Official references checked: 13 October 2026 · Singapore

This guide follows a typical residential tenancy approaching its agreed end date. The suggested 6–8 week lead time is not a legal requirement. For early termination or holding over, the agreement and facts may require a different process.

What can a landlord deduct from the deposit?

Not every mark or breakdown is automatically a tenant charge. Start with the TA, the original condition report, fair wear and tear, the cause of the issue and any agreed repair or reinstatement obligations.

For example, a new stain caused during the tenancy may be treated differently from a pre-existing mark or ordinary ageing. An itemised quote or invoice and before-and-after photos make any deduction easier to assess. Do not assume that the full cost of replacing an old item can always be charged.

Keep these records together

Before moving out

Signed TA, original inventory and photos, cleaning/servicing requirements, repair approvals and contractor receipts.

At handover

Dated photos/videos, inspection notes, key/access-card count, meter readings and written acknowledgement.

After handover

Final bills, any deduction breakdown, supporting invoices, deposit refund record and important messages.

If there is a disagreement

Identify the exact disputed item, relevant clause and evidence. Seek an agreed resolution before escalating.

REAL-WORLD RENTAL SCENARIO

What should happen in the final month before handing back the keys?

A tenant has booked movers but forgotten the aircon servicing receipt, curtain-cleaning requirement and utility closure. On handover day, both sides rush through the inventory and later disagree over deductions.

What to do next

  1. Read the notice, reinstatement, cleaning and handover clauses early.
  2. Schedule movers, any required servicing and professional cleaning with receipts.
  3. Photograph each room, appliances, fittings and meter readings before key return.
  4. Inspect together where possible, record outstanding items and confirm how deposit reconciliation will work.

JC’s practical takeaway: Do not assume every tenancy requires professional cleaning or curtain dry-cleaning; check the signed agreement.

JC’S PRACTICAL TIP

Use the same inventory list from move-in when inspecting at move-out. Note each item as returned, missing, damaged or requiring further checks. Take photos before any cleaning or repair work changes the condition.

PUT THIS GUIDE INTO PRACTICE

Your final handover action list

Illustrative scenario: Keys returned, but a fault still needs checking

An appliance cannot be fully checked at handover. Record the observed symptoms and any outstanding inspection clearly. Avoid treating a note that an item needs checking as automatic acceptance of liability.

Who should do what?

  • Tenant: arrange the cleaning and servicing required by the agreement and retain receipts.
  • Landlord: compare the final condition with the inventory and move-in records, and identify concerns specifically.
  • Both: count keys, cards and remotes together; record meter readings, outstanding items and the deposit follow-up date.
