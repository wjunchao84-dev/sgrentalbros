Tenancy Agreement in Singapore

A practical checklist of what landlords and tenants should understand before signing a rental contract.

Important: This guide is for general information, not legal, tax or professional advice. Rules may change, and your signed agreement and circumstances matter. Check official sources before acting. Read the full disclaimer →
IN PLAIN ENGLISH

A tenancy agreement records the landlord's and tenant's agreed rights and obligations. Singapore's Council for Estate Agencies (CEA) provides residential tenancy templates, but the parties may negotiate the terms before signing.

Sources checked: 9 October 2026 · Singapore

01

Check the essentials

Confirm the correct parties and property, rental amount, payment date, tenancy dates, security deposit, approved occupants, permitted use, furnishing and inventory. Verify bank details independently before transferring money.

02

Read the clauses that cause disputes

Focus on repairs and repair caps, defects at handover, aircon servicing, cleaning, access for viewings, pets, early termination, renewal, deposit deductions and handover obligations. A sample clause is not automatically part of your contract.

03

Know what is law versus agreement

Regulatory requirements such as HDB approval, URA minimum-stay rules and IRAS stamp duty apply independently of what the parties write. Other matters, such as a diplomatic clause or a particular minor-repair cap, depend on the agreed wording.

04

Before and after signing

Ask questions and negotiate unclear clauses before signing. Keep a copy signed by all parties, the inventory and condition photos, payment receipts, the Stamp Certificate and any written changes.

REAL-WORLD RENTAL SCENARIO

“It’s a standard tenancy agreement.” Should you sign immediately?

A tenant agrees on rent and move-in date, but the draft contract includes a no-renewal condition, a repair-cost cap and a requirement to allow viewings before the lease ends. These conditions can affect the tenancy even when the rent is acceptable.

JC’s checklist before signing

  1. Confirm the parties, property address, tenancy dates, monthly rent, deposit and approved occupants.
  2. Read the repair, aircon servicing, access, early termination, renewal, cleaning and deposit deduction clauses carefully.
  3. Compare the contract with the offer, inventory and any written promises. Ask for agreed changes to be reflected in the signed documents.
  4. Keep the signed agreement, inventory, condition photos, payment records and applicable stamp duty documents.

JC’s practical takeaway: “Standard” does not mean every clause is compulsory or suitable for your situation. Understand what you are agreeing to before signing.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Singapore tenancy agreement FAQs

Is a diplomatic clause automatically included?

No. Whether an early-termination or diplomatic clause applies depends on the actual agreement. Check its conditions, notice requirements and any reimbursement obligations.

Does the tenant always pay the first S$200 or S$300 for repairs?

No universal S$200 or S$300 minor-repair cap applies to every private tenancy. Check the signed repair clause, what caused the defect and any relevant exceptions.

Who pays stamp duty on a tenancy agreement?

Stamp duty obligations should be checked against IRAS rules and the contract. Do not assume that the party arranging the paperwork is automatically responsible for the cost.

Can a WhatsApp promise override a clause in the signed agreement?

Messages can be relevant evidence, but conflicting terms create unnecessary disputes. Put agreed permissions and changes clearly into the signed agreement or an appropriate written amendment.

JC’S PRACTICAL TIP

Ask both parties to walk through the repair, termination, access and deposit clauses together. Most misunderstandings arise from assuming the other party reads them the same way.

Is the CEA tenancy template compulsory?

No. The Council for Estate Agencies provides residential tenancy templates as a guide. Landlords and tenants are free to negotiate the terms before signing. A template clause does not automatically apply unless it forms part of the agreement the parties enter into.

Practical check: Ask which terms are required by law and which are simply proposed contractual conditions. That distinction matters for repair limits, diplomatic clauses, access and deposit deductions.
