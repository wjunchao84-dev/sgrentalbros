Tenancy Agreement

What should you check before signing?

Important: This guide is for general information, not legal, tax or professional advice. Rules may change, and your signed agreement and circumstances matter. Check official sources before acting. Read the full disclaimer →
IN PLAIN ENGLISH

A tenancy agreement records the landlord's and tenant's agreed rights and obligations. Singapore's Council for Estate Agencies (CEA) provides residential tenancy templates, but the parties may negotiate the terms before signing.

Sources checked: 9 October 2026 · Singapore

01

Check the essentials

Confirm the correct parties and property, rental amount, payment date, tenancy dates, security deposit, approved occupants, permitted use, furnishing and inventory. Verify bank details independently before transferring money.

02

Read the clauses that cause disputes

Focus on repairs and repair caps, defects at handover, aircon servicing, cleaning, access for viewings, pets, early termination, renewal, deposit deductions and handover obligations. A sample clause is not automatically part of your contract.

03

Know what is law versus agreement

Regulatory requirements such as HDB approval, URA minimum-stay rules and IRAS stamp duty apply independently of what the parties write. Other matters, such as a diplomatic clause or a particular minor-repair cap, depend on the agreed wording.

04

Before and after signing

Ask questions and negotiate unclear clauses before signing. Keep a copy signed by all parties, the inventory and condition photos, payment receipts, the Stamp Certificate and any written changes.

JC’S PRACTICAL TIP

Ask both parties to walk through the repair, termination, access and deposit clauses together. Most misunderstandings arise from assuming the other party reads them the same way.
