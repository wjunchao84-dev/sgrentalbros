Landlord's Responsibilities During Tenancy

What Singapore landlords should do from handover until the tenant moves out.

Important: This guide is for general educational information, not legal, tax or professional advice. Some duties come from law or property regulations; others depend on the signed tenancy agreement (TA). Read the full disclaimer →
IN PLAIN ENGLISH

A good landlord honours the agreement, responds to problems, keeps proper records, respects the tenant's occupancy and follows the property's regulatory requirements.

Official references checked: 10 October 2026 · Singapore

This checklist covers common private residential rentals, with HDB-specific obligations highlighted. “TA” means tenancy agreement. A practice commonly used by agents is not automatically a statutory requirement.

01. Honour the signed tenancyThroughout leaseProvide the premises and agreed items for the tenancy period, and follow the signed terms. Do not assume you can change rent, shorten the lease or demand vacant possession without an agreed or lawful basis.TA / law
02. Keep rental and payment recordsEvery paymentRecord rent received, receipts, outstanding amounts and agreed changes. Follow the TA if payment is late; avoid making up penalties or changing payment terms unilaterally.TA / records
03. Respond to repair reportsWhen notifiedAcknowledge faults promptly. Arrange inspection or a contractor where appropriate, especially for leaks, electrical hazards and loss of essential facilities. Determine cost responsibility using the cause and agreed clauses.TA / safety
04. Handle landlord-side repairsWhen requiredPay for repairs or replacements allocated to the landlord under the TA, including agreed responsibilities for structural issues, pre-existing defects or major equipment. No single rule makes the landlord pay for every breakdown.TA / cause
05. Apply repair caps fairlyFor each repairCheck whether a minor-repair cap applies, whether exclusions exist and who authorises the work. Keep contractor findings and invoices; distinguish fair wear and tear from misuse.TA
06. Respect access and privacyFor inspections, works or viewingsGive the notice and arrange appointments as required by the TA, except where emergency access may be justified. Ownership does not create an unrestricted right to enter an occupied home.TA / circumstances
07. Maintain required approvals and eligibilityBefore and during HDB rentalFor HDB rentals, obtain approval before renting out the flat or bedrooms and comply with tenant eligibility, occupancy and registration conditions. Notify HDB of relevant changes within the prescribed period.HDB rules
08. Follow private-home occupancy and use rulesThroughout leaseFor private homes, check URA minimum-stay, occupancy and permitted-use requirements. Do not approve unlawful short-term stays or unauthorised subletting.URA rules
09. Keep condominium matters coordinatedAs neededShare relevant estate rules, coordinate management access and follow MCST requirements for moving, renovation or facilities. Allocate maintenance charges and other costs according to the TA.MCST / TA
10. Deal with utilities correctlyAt move-in and move-outCoordinate closure or transfer of existing utility accounts and opening of the new account. Do not assume the tenant must pay utilities if the TA says they are included.TA
11. Manage tenant requests in writingWhen requests ariseRespond to requests about pets, alterations, extra occupants, repairs and early termination. Confirm any consent, refusal or variation clearly in writing.TA / regulation
12. Account for the security depositAt lease end and when deductions ariseUse the deposit only as permitted by the agreement and applicable law. Compare inventory and dated photos, explain any deductions, and return the balance within the contractual timeframe.TA

Repairs: landlord or tenant?

Check the cause and the signed clause. The landlord may be responsible for a structural defect or a major replacement allocated to them by the agreement. The tenant may have to pay for misuse or agreed minor repairs. The cost cap, exclusions, approval process and any defect-reporting period vary by TA.

For example, if a sink is blocked, obtain a contractor's diagnosis where necessary. Do not automatically charge the tenant or landlord without checking the cause and agreed terms.

Simple landlord management routine

Monthly

Check rent receipts and respond to maintenance messages. Keep clear records of repairs and expenses.

When something breaks

Acknowledge the issue, assess urgency, appoint a suitable contractor and document responsibility and approval.

When occupants or terms change

Check HDB/URA requirements and the TA. Record approvals or variations in writing.

Before lease expiry

Check renewal terms, agree handover arrangements and prepare a documented deposit reconciliation.

REAL-WORLD RENTAL SCENARIO

A tenant reports a recurring appliance fault. What should the landlord do?

A dryer was repaired before the tenancy but develops the same sound again. The landlord wants the tenant to pay under the minor-repair clause, while the tenant points to the old repair history. A documented inspection can help prevent a small problem becoming a dispute.

What to do next

  1. Acknowledge the report and check whether the issue presents a safety risk.
  2. Review the signed agreement and previous repair or handover records.
  3. Arrange a suitable contractor's diagnosis and explain the proposed solution.
  4. Confirm responsibility, access arrangements and any agreed cost sharing in writing.

JC’s practical takeaway: Prompt, clear communication and a paper trail are often as important as the repair itself.

JC’S PRACTICAL TIP

Keep one property file with the signed TA, HDB approval if applicable, handover photos, rent ledger, contractor receipts and important tenant messages. It saves time and helps resolve disputes fairly.

When a tenant reports a faulty appliance

Use a consistent process instead of deciding who pays before finding out what happened.

  1. Record the report. Ask for photos or a video, when the problem began and whether it is safe to use.
  2. Check the history. Review the handover report, past repairs and servicing records.
  3. Arrange a diagnosis. Coordinate access and obtain a quotation where needed.
  4. Review responsibility. Apply the signed agreement, including repair limits and exceptions, to the evidence.
  5. Confirm the outcome. Put the agreed work, cost allocation and completion details in writing.

Example: a cooker hood fails again

Suppose the hood buttons were repaired before the tenant moved in but stop working again later. A pre-existing issue, ordinary usage and the precise repair clause may all matter. Neither party should assume that the repair cap alone decides responsibility.

JC's tip: Keep one clear record of each issue so both parties can see what was reported, approved and completed.
