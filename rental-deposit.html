Rental Deposit

What it secures and how deductions may arise.

Important: This guide is for general information, not legal, tax or professional advice. Rules may change, and your signed agreement and circumstances matter. Check official sources before acting. Read the full disclaimer →
IN PLAIN ENGLISH

A security deposit is money held as security for obligations under the tenancy. The amount, permitted deductions and return process depend on the signed agreement; there is no universal rule that every Singapore rental requires exactly one or two months' deposit.

Sources checked: 9 October 2026 · Singapore

01

What should the deposit cover?

Read the clause: it may address unpaid rent, utilities, damage beyond fair wear and tear or other agreed liabilities. Do not assume every cleaning or replacement cost can be deducted.

02

How much is required?

One month's deposit for a one-year lease and two months for a two-year lease are common market arrangements, not a mandatory statutory formula for every private tenancy.

03

At handover

Compare the inventory and move-in condition report with dated move-out photos. Distinguish fair wear and tear from damage, and request invoices or a clear calculation for disputed deductions.

04

When is it returned?

Check the contract for the refund timeline and any conditions. The landlord and tenant should document the final accounting and agree on any outstanding bills or repairs.

REAL-WORLD RENTAL SCENARIO

Can a landlord deduct cleaning, repainting or appliance replacement from the deposit?

At move-out, a sofa shows light wear, a tap is loose and the oven no longer works. The landlord wants to replace everything; the tenant says it is ordinary wear and tear. A fair assessment starts with the signed terms and evidence, not a blanket deduction.

What to do next

  1. Compare move-in and move-out condition reports and dated photographs.
  2. Separate normal ageing from damage, missing items and unpaid obligations.
  3. Ask for itemised quotations or invoices and consider the age and condition of items.
  4. Confirm the agreed refund deadline and send a clear written breakdown of any deduction.

JC’s practical takeaway: A security deposit is not an automatic renovation fund. Deductions must be supportable under the tenancy and facts.

BEFORE DEDUCTING FROM THE DEPOSIT

A practical five-step checklist for landlords and tenants

  1. Read the signed tenancy agreement for the deposit refund timeline and permitted deductions.
  2. Compare the move-in inventory and photographs with the move-out inspection.
  3. Identify each disputed item separately: missing property, cleaning, damage or unpaid bills.
  4. Request dated evidence and an itemised quotation or invoice; consider existing condition and fair wear and tear.
  5. Confirm any agreed deduction and the balance to be returned in writing.

A disagreement about one item does not mean every other part of the handover must remain unclear. Keep a written record of what is agreed and what is disputed.

JC’S PRACTICAL TIP

Photograph the unit and inventory together at move-in and move-out. A clear condition record is more useful than a disagreement over what is 'normal'.

Deposit deduction: a practical decision checklist

Before a landlord proposes a deduction, compare the signed agreement, the move-in condition record and the move-out evidence. A charge should not be assumed merely because an item looks older than it did at move-in.

  1. Identify the item: What exactly is damaged, missing or unclean?
  2. Compare evidence: Check dated photos, inventory notes and earlier defect reports.
  3. Check the clause: Does the signed agreement support this type of deduction, including any fair-wear-and-tear exception?
  4. Support the amount: Obtain an itemised invoice or reasonable quotation and explain the calculation.
  5. Settle transparently: Send a written breakdown and follow the contractual return timeline for any balance.

Example: old aircon versus accidental damage

An aircon that fails through age is different from a unit damaged by misuse or neglected servicing. The repair report, service records and contractual wording can affect responsibility. Neither party should assume that every breakdown can be charged to the deposit.
