Handover & Inventory

Protect both sides from day one.

Important: This guide is for general information, not legal, tax or professional advice. Rules may change, and your signed agreement and circumstances matter. Check official sources before acting. Read the full disclaimer →
IN PLAIN ENGLISH

A handover report should show what was provided, what condition it was in, what was returned and what both parties agreed to check or repair.

Practical guide updated: 13 October 2026 · Singapore

Use the original inventory and condition report for both move-in and move-out. A good report reduces arguments about pre-existing defects, missing items and security deposit deductions. The signed tenancy agreement (TA) governs the parties' obligations.

01

Record everything handed over

List furniture, appliances, fittings, aircon remotes, door keys, letterbox keys, access cards, parking transponders and any digital-lock codes or devices. Record quantities and serial numbers only where useful and appropriate.

02

Photograph condition, not just objects

Take clear dated overview and close-up photos. Capture existing stains, scratches, chips, cracks, water marks and defects. Label photos with the location and issue, for example “Kitchen countertop — two pre-existing stains”.

03

Check appliances and utilities

Where safe and practical, check that the aircon, lights, taps, appliances and supplied remotes operate. Record meter readings and utility account arrangements. Report anything untested rather than assuming it works.

04

Allow the agreed defect-reporting process

At move-in, record issues that may not be visible during a short inspection. Follow any defect reporting or warranty period in the TA. A one-month defect period is a common negotiated term, not a universal legal rule.

05

Compare the same list at move-out

Tick items as returned, missing, damaged, pre-existing or needing further checks. Distinguish fair wear and tear from tenant-caused damage and consider contractor findings before allocating repair costs.

06

Sign off what is actually agreed

Record the date and time, who attended, keys returned, any disputed findings and any agreed follow-up. Both parties should have a copy. A signature acknowledging the inspection does not have to mean agreement with every disputed deduction.

PRACTICAL RENTAL SCENARIO

Why does a detailed inventory matter?

A lamp is said to be missing at move-out, but the tenant says it was never supplied.

What to do next

  1. List keys, cards and fittings.
  2. Take dated photos.
  3. Compare the same record at move-out.

JC’s practical takeaway: An acknowledged inventory helps both sides.

JC'S PRACTICAL TIP

For every handover photo, write a simple caption: item + location + condition. For example, “Master bedroom wardrobe — right sliding door slightly misaligned”. Clear captions make later comparisons much easier.

Before the tenant returns the keys

Ask the tenant to review the Move-Out Checklist →, including the hood grease filter, washing-machine rubber seal, receipts for any required aircon or curtain servicing, utility coordination and the complete inventory. See the End-of-Tenancy Timeline → for the full process.

Deposit deductions: record any issue first, then reconcile it fairly against the TA and supporting evidence. Do not promise a full refund before the final inspection and accounts are completed.

REAL RENTAL SITUATION

What if the inventory says an appliance works but it does not?

At move-in, the dryer runs but makes a strange sound. Neither party records the noise, creating disagreement months later.

What I would check next

  1. Test appliances, lights, taps, locks and remotes during handover.
  2. Photograph and video the condition, including any unusual sounds.
  3. Record exceptions in the inventory and send them promptly in writing.
  4. Keep signed copies and agree how missing or defective items will be addressed.

JC's takeaway: Clear communication and a dated written record help both sides avoid preventable disagreements.

REAL-LIFE RENTAL QUESTION

The unit looks fine at handover. Why take so many photos?

A scratch on the countertop is noticed at move-out, but nobody can tell whether it was already there.

What I would check first

Photograph walls, floors, furniture, appliance operation and existing defects; record meter readings and key counts; share the report promptly.

JC's practical takeaway: A detailed, dated inventory protects both landlord and tenant better than memory.
