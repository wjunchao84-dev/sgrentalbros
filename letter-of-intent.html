Letter of Intent

What the LOI does before the tenancy agreement.

Important: This guide is for general information, not legal, tax or professional advice. Rules may change, and your signed agreement and circumstances matter. Check official sources before acting. Read the full disclaimer →
THE SIMPLE VERSION

An LOI commonly records the proposed commercial terms before the full tenancy agreement is finalised. Be clear about what has been offered, what remains subject to agreement and how any accompanying payment is treated.

01

Start with the document

Check the signed tenancy agreement, inventory and any written amendments or messages that form part of the arrangement. The exact facts and wording matter.

02

Keep evidence

Keep relevant photos, receipts, payment records and messages. Good records make it much easier to establish what happened and what was agreed.

03

Resolve it clearly

Communicate the issue and proposed solution clearly. If both sides agree to change an existing arrangement, record that agreement in writing.

JC'S PRACTICAL TIP

Don't rely on what is “usually done” when the signed agreement says something specific. Read the clause first, then deal with the actual situation.

REAL RENTAL SITUATION

Does an accepted LOI mean everything is final?

A tenant pays a good-faith deposit after the LOI is accepted, then discovers that the requested early-termination clause is absent from the draft tenancy agreement.

What I would check next

  1. Check whether the LOI is binding and what conditions it states.
  2. Record key terms such as rent, commencement, furnishings, pets and any special clauses.
  3. Clarify what happens to money paid if the agreement is not signed.
  4. Have both parties confirm changes before signing the tenancy agreement.

JC's takeaway: Keep the agreement, supporting records and decisions clear in writing. Common market practice is not automatically a legal requirement.

REAL-LIFE RENTAL QUESTION

Does paying a good-faith deposit mean the tenancy is final?

A prospective tenant pays money after signing an LOI, then discovers the tenancy agreement contains terms not discussed earlier.

What I would check first

Specify rent, term, start date, furnishings, conditions and how the payment is handled; identify terms still subject to agreement; keep written records.

JC's practical takeaway: Do not assume every LOI has the same legal effect. The wording and circumstances matter.
