Pets in a Rental

Get the permission and conditions clear.

Important: This guide is for general information, not legal, tax or professional advice. Rules may change, and your signed agreement and circumstances matter. Check official sources before acting. Read the full disclaimer →
THE SIMPLE VERSION

Do not assume a pet is permitted. Check the tenancy terms, property rules and any specific conditions agreed with the landlord before moving a pet into the property.

01

Start with the document

Check the signed tenancy agreement, inventory and any written amendments or messages that form part of the arrangement. The exact facts and wording matter.

02

Keep evidence

Keep relevant photos, receipts, payment records and messages. Good records make it much easier to establish what happened and what was agreed.

03

Resolve it clearly

Communicate the issue and proposed solution clearly. If both sides agree to change an existing arrangement, record that agreement in writing.

PRACTICAL RENTAL SCENARIO

Condo allows pets. Is landlord consent still needed?

A tenant adopts a dog without checking the tenancy.

What to do next

  1. Check the tenancy and building rules.
  2. Seek written permission if required.
  3. Agree on damage and cleaning expectations.

JC’s practical takeaway: Condo rules and landlord consent are different issues.

JC'S PRACTICAL TIP

Don't rely on what is “usually done” when the signed agreement says something specific. Read the clause first, then deal with the actual situation.

REAL RENTAL SITUATION

The condo allows dogs. Does that mean my landlord must allow mine?

A tenant checks the building's pet rules but forgets that the tenancy agreement requires landlord consent.

What I would check next

  1. Check both the tenancy agreement and relevant building or estate rules.
  2. Get permission in writing before bringing in a pet where required.
  3. Agree any cleaning, damage and nuisance responsibilities.
  4. Document the unit's starting condition and any agreed pet-related terms.

JC's takeaway: Check the applicable rules and signed agreement, then confirm decisions in writing.

REAL-LIFE RENTAL QUESTION

Can a tenant keep a pet if the condo allows pets?

A tenant adopts a dog because the condominium rules permit pets, but the tenancy agreement requires landlord consent.

What I would check first

Check the tenancy pet clause and applicable building or HDB rules; seek written permission; agree cleaning and damage responsibilities.

JC's practical takeaway: Building permission does not automatically replace the landlord's contractual consent.
