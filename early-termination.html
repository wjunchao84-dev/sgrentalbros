Early Termination

What if someone needs to leave early?

Important: This guide is for general information, not legal, tax or professional advice. Rules may change, and your signed agreement and circumstances matter. Check official sources before acting. Read the full disclaimer →
IN PLAIN ENGLISH

A fixed-term lease does not automatically end just because a landlord or tenant wants to leave early. Start with the signed termination provisions and any applicable legal rights; if no agreed exit route applies, negotiate a written surrender rather than assuming notice alone is enough.

Sources checked: 9 October 2026 · Singapore

01

Check the contract first

Look for a diplomatic clause, break clause, minimum occupation period, notice method, required evidence and any agreed reimbursement of agency fees. Read the whole clause, including exceptions.

02

If there is no applicable break clause

Contact the other party early and propose a mutually agreed surrender. Discuss the intended exit date, rent, deposit treatment, marketing/viewings and any documented costs. Agreement is not guaranteed.

03

Put the settlement in writing

Record the agreed final date, keys, access, inspection, outstanding bills, deposit accounting and whether further claims are released. Both parties should confirm the same terms.

04

Avoid common assumptions

A one-month or two-month notice is not a universal right to terminate a fixed lease. Nor does paying a security deposit automatically buy the right to leave early.

PRACTICAL RENTAL SCENARIO

What if you must leave without a break clause?

A tenant wants to leave early, but the lease has no exit option.

What to do next

  1. Read the signed agreement.
  2. Discuss written surrender or replacement arrangements.
  3. Record the final settlement.

JC’s practical takeaway: Moving out alone does not end the lease.

JC’S PRACTICAL TIP

Before accepting an early handover, make sure the agreement says what happens to the remaining rent and deposit. Don't leave either point to assumptions.

Three routes to ending a lease early

  1. An applicable break clause: Check the qualifying event, notice period and any documents required by the agreement.
  2. Mutual agreement: If no break clause applies, both parties can discuss whether to agree on an earlier end date and record their settlement in writing.
  3. A possible contractual breach: Document the facts and seek appropriate advice before assuming the tenancy can be terminated.

What to record if both parties agree to end early

Confirm the final tenancy date, rent up to that date, key handover, cleaning and repair arrangements, deposit reconciliation and any negotiated compensation.

Practical takeaway: Moving out does not necessarily end contractual obligations. Follow the agreement or reach a clearly documented settlement.

REAL RENTAL SITUATION

Can I just give one month's notice and move out?

A tenant finds a new job and wants to leave early, but the agreement has no general break clause.

What I would check next

  1. Look for a break or diplomatic clause rather than assuming one exists.
  2. Discuss a mutually agreed surrender if no contractual exit applies.
  3. Record any agreed replacement-tenant, rent or reasonable costs arrangements.
  4. Confirm key return and final accounting in a signed written agreement.

JC's takeaway: Read the signed terms, document the facts and agree practical next steps in writing. Contract terms and legal requirements are not the same thing.

REAL-LIFE RENTAL QUESTION

What if a tenant wants to leave before the lease expires?

A tenant needs to move out six months early, but the agreement contains no usable early-exit provision.

What I would check first

Read termination and breach clauses; calculate potential obligations; discuss a documented mutual surrender; consider replacement-tenant arrangements only if agreed.

JC's practical takeaway: Do not assume giving two months' notice always ends a fixed-term lease.
