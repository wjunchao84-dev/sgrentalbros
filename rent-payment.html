Rent Payments

Dates, records and avoiding payment confusion.

Important: This guide is for general information, not legal, tax or professional advice. Rules may change, and your signed agreement and circumstances matter. Check official sources before acting. Read the full disclaimer →
THE SIMPLE VERSION

Record the agreed rent, due date, payment account and any change of payment instructions. Keep transfer records and verify new instructions carefully, especially if ownership of the property changes.

01

Start with the document

Check the signed tenancy agreement, inventory and any written amendments or messages that form part of the arrangement. The exact facts and wording matter.

02

Keep evidence

Keep relevant photos, receipts, payment records and messages. Good records make it much easier to establish what happened and what was agreed.

03

Resolve it clearly

Communicate the issue and proposed solution clearly. If both sides agree to change an existing arrangement, record that agreement in writing.

REAL-LIFE SINGAPORE RENTAL EXAMPLE

What if the rent arrives late?

A standing bank instruction fails, and the landlord has not received rent on the agreed due date.

What I would do

Check the tenancy agreement for the due date and any grace period; notify the other party promptly; arrange payment through the verified account and retain the bank reference.

JC's practical tip: A grace period, late fee or termination right should not be assumed. Review the contract and applicable law before taking action.

Frequently asked questions

Can a tenant use the security deposit to pay the last month's rent? Not automatically. The deposit usually secures obligations under the tenancy; using it as rent needs the landlord's agreement or a contractual right.

Can a landlord change the bank account for rent? Payment instructions can change, but the tenant should independently verify the change and keep written confirmation before paying.

Legal requirement vs agreement: Common market practice is not automatically law. Check your signed documents and current official guidance before acting.
